# Contact dermatitis — summary

> Summarized in our own words from NHS and AAD contact-dermatitis guidance for RAG grounding.
> See the full guidance at the source URL above.

## What it is

Contact dermatitis is a type of eczema triggered when the skin touches an irritant substance or something that causes an allergic reaction. The rash usually appears on the area of skin that had contact.

## Typical symptoms

The affected area may become red, itchy, dry, cracked or scaly, and sometimes swollen or blistered. Symptoms often develop within hours or days of contact.

## Common causes

Irritant contact dermatitis can follow exposure to soaps, detergents, solvents, or frequent hand washing. Allergic contact dermatitis is linked to substances such as nickel, fragrances, latex, or certain preservatives in skincare products.

## General care

Guidance generally covers identifying and avoiding the trigger, keeping the area moisturized with gentle products, avoiding scented soaps and harsh detergents, and using cool compresses to soothe irritation. Medication options should be discussed with a clinician or pharmacist.

## When to get professional care

Seek professional advice if the rash is severe, spreading, painful, shows signs of infection, or keeps returning. Only a qualified professional can identify the cause.
