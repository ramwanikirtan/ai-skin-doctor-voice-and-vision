# Eczema (atopic dermatitis) — summary

> Summarized in our own words from the AAD eczema pages for RAG grounding.
> See the full guidance at the source URL above.

## What it is

Atopic dermatitis is a common chronic inflammatory skin condition. It often appears as dry, red, itchy patches and can flare up and calm down over time. It frequently starts in childhood but can occur at any age.

## Typical symptoms

Itching is usually the most noticeable symptom. Affected skin may look dry, red or darker than surrounding skin, rough or scaly, and may develop small raised bumps. Scratching can make irritation worse and break the skin.

## Common triggers

Flare-ups are often linked to dry skin, irritants such as harsh soaps or detergents, stress, heat and sweating, and certain fabrics. Identifying personal triggers can help reduce how often flares occur.

## General care

Gentle skin care usually includes regular moisturizing, brief lukewarm baths or showers, mild fragrance-free cleansers, and avoiding known irritants. Over-the-counter options exist, but treatment choices should be discussed with a clinician or pharmacist.

## When to get professional care

See a dermatologist if itching interferes with sleep or daily life, the rash spreads or looks infected, or self-care measures do not help. Only a qualified professional can diagnose the condition.
